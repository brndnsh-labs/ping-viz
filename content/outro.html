Round trip

Mike Muuss wrote ping in December 1983 to chase down odd behavior on his network, "a little thousand-line hack that I wrote in an evening", and named it after the sound a sonar makes. It still works the same way.

Every request in Act II rides on everything in Act I: the same interrupts, the same packets with the same headers, the same routers subtracting one from the same time to live. The interview answer used to end at the far side of the network. Now that's where most of the work happens.

Notes

  • The house, the route and the timings are one plausible run. The addresses inside the house are ordinary private ones (RFC 1918). Its public address and the hardware addresses come from ranges reserved for documentation (RFC 5737, RFC 7042). google.com's address and name are real, from a lookup in October 2026.
  • The packet bytes are exact: checksums and all, as Linux's ping would send them.
  • Everything about Anthropic's infrastructure is public: the chip platforms and capacity are from Anthropic's and Amazon's announcements. The address, network and front door are from DNS, BGP and the API's own response headers, in October 2026. The datacenter on the globe sits at Project Rainier's public site in New Carlisle, Indiana; the datacenter and model pictures are illustrations, not floor plans or architecture diagrams.
